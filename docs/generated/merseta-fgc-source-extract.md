1. Workshop mandate
The section of the workshop which is FGC Probing, will determine, document and validate the
current level of learner utilisation and lecturer independence across participating TVET
colleges. It will use platform records, college evidence and observed practical
demonstrations to produce defensible findings for the project report.
FGC Question 1: When are learners using the platform?
FGC Question 2: Do college lecturers know how to independently create curriculum-
relevant experiences and publish them on the EON Platform for teaching and
learning?
2. Validation outcomes
• Establish actual learner-use patterns by college, campus, programme, subject,
cohort, day and time.
• Distinguish scheduled classroom use, lecturer-facilitated use, independent learner
use, assessment/project use and non-use.
• Identify utilisation barriers such as licences, connectivity, devices, timetable
integration, access support and inactive accounts.
• Verify lecturer capability through an individual build–publish–assign–demonstrate
task, rather than self-report alone.
• Classify lecturer capability as Independent, Partially Independent/Support
Required, or Not Yet Demonstrated.
• Agree a 90-day activation and reinforcement plan with accountable owners,
dates and measurable targets.
3. Mandatory pre-work evidence pack
Each college must submit the following before the workshop. Where EON controls the
relevant analytics, EON must extract and prepare them at college and campus level.
Evidence source Minimum requirement
Platform analytics Logins, active users, session dates/times, frequency, duration where
available, experiences accessed, completions, assignments,
author/publisher activity, and inactive licences for an agreed
reporting period.
College utilisation Class timetables, lesson plans, lecturer logs, learner registers,
evidence subject/programme mapping, assignments and samples of learner
work.

6. Lecturer capability assessment standard
A lecturer should only be recorded as independently capable when the evidence shows that
the lecturer personally completed the full workflow with no facilitator taking control of the
task.
Capability area Observable evidence
1. Relevance and lesson Selects a genuine programme/subject need, states learning
design outcomes and aligns the experience to curriculum and learner
level.
2. Experience creation Creates or adapts an experience; adds suitable assets/content,
interactions and explanatory guidance.
3. Platform management Names, organises, tags and saves the experience correctly
under the lecturer’s own account.
4. Publishing and Publishes/loads the experience, shares it appropriately and
deployment assigns it to a learner/test group.
5. Teaching application Explains and demonstrates how the experience will be used
before, during or after a lesson.
6. Learner evidence and Shows how learner participation, progress, completion or
analytics assessment evidence will be reviewed.
7. Troubleshooting and Resolves routine issues or explains the correct support route and
repeatability can repeat the workflow for another topic.
Capability classifications
• Independent: completes the end-to-end workflow without facilitator intervention
and meets the minimum quality standard.
• Partially Independent / Support Required: completes most steps but needs limited
prompting or technical assistance; targeted reinforcement and reassessment are
required.
• Not Yet Demonstrated: cannot complete or evidence the end-to-end workflow;
structured support and a later practical reassessment are required.

Important reporting rule: Training attendance, a certificate, confidence survey or
group-created output is not sufficient proof of independent lecturer capability.
7. Final workshop deliverables
• FGC Question 1 evidence matrix: when learners use EON, disaggregated by
college/campus/programme/cohort and supported by analytics plus college
records.
• FGC Question 2 lecturer capability register: individual evidence, assessor rating,
moderation result and support requirement.
• Repository/index of published lecturer-created experiences with author, curriculum
topic, date, URL/ID and intended learner cohort.
• Utilisation barriers, risks and corrective-action register, including inactive licence
activation or reallocation where justified.
• College-level 30/60/90-day activation and reinforcement plan with measurable
targets, owners and follow-up dates.
• Report-ready narrative, annexure index, attendance registers and
photographic/video evidence, subject to consent and governance requirements.
8. Recommended answer format for the FGC report
The final report should not give an unsupported “yes/no” response. It should state the verified
finding, quantify the evidence, identify variation between colleges, disclose gaps and specify
corrective actions.
Question 1 reporting sentence: During the verified reporting period, the lecturer
demonstrated that learners used EON predominantly [eThekwini Workshop], across
[Engineering Studies], supported by [analytics and documentary evidence]. Usage
was limited or absent in [areas], mainly because of [verified causes].
Question 2 reporting sentence: Of the [number] lecturers assessed individually,
[number/%] demonstrated independent end-to-end creation and publishing
capability; [number/%] required limited support; and [number/%] did not yet
demonstrate the required capability. The conclusion is supported by live build
outputs, author/publish records, assignment proof and moderated assessment
rubrics.

Facilitator controls and evidence rules
Completion contract
• Every conclusion must point to identifiable evidence.
• Platform analytics must be reconciled with college records; neither source should stand
alone where discrepancies exist.
• Lecturer capability must be assessed individually under observation.
• No facilitator may take control of a lecturer’s creation or publishing task.
• Missing evidence must be labelled “Not Verified”, not assumed.
• Findings must show variation between colleges and avoid an unsupported blanket yes/no
conclusion.

Evidence rules, roles and workshop discipline
Non-negotiable evidence rules
• Every conclusion must point to identifiable, retrievable evidence.
• Platform analytics must be reconciled with college records where the question requires
context or confirmation.

lecturer’s own account.
• Facilitator execution invalidates independence for the affected step.
• Training attendance, certification, confidence, self-report and group output are not
substitutes for performance evidence.
• Current findings and future commitments must be reported separately.

CAPABILITY AND REINFORCEMENT PLAN
DAY 2 | Primary focus: FGC Question 2 | Timing: confirm in approved run sheet
Session purpose: Create a 30/60/90-day system for strengthening lecturer capability and
verifying that skills transfer into repeated teaching practice.
Required preparation and materials
• Capability register and gap analysis
• Academic calendar
• Train-the-Trainer/XR Ambassador arrangements
• Helpdesk and 9-Step Reinforcement routes
• 30/60/90-day template
Facilitator procedure
1. Aggregate common gaps without losing individual reassessment needs.
2. Assign support through the appropriate route: Train-the-Trainer, XR Ambassador, helpdesk,
content review or 9-Step Reinforcement.
3. Set milestones for reassessment, a second independently created experience and verified
classroom deployment.
4. Name college management, platform lead, mentor, assessor and evidence owner
responsibilities.
5. Define measures at 30, 60 and 90 days and schedule review dates.
6. Confirm escalation when milestones or evidence are missed.
Participant actions
• Commit to individual and college actions.
• Confirm availability of mentors, timetable opportunities and test/learner groups.
Required evidence and output
Evidence/output Minimum acceptance standard
Capability plan Milestones, measures, owners and dates.
Reassessment schedule Lecturers, tasks and assessors.
Reinforcement evidence Expected new experiences, assignments and use records.
Facilitation cautions and decision rules
• A support plan must lead to fresh independent evidence, not only additional attendance.
• Separate technical support from pedagogical/content-quality support.
Completion gate: Close when every gap has a reinforcement path and every follow-up
decision has a measure, owner and date.

FGC EVIDENCE CONSOLIDATION AND CLOSE
DAY 2 | Primary focus: Both FGC questions | Timing: confirm in approved run sheet
Session purpose: Consolidate the two validated findings, confirm evidence ownership and
deadlines, and leave with a report-ready index rather than a collection of disconnected workshop
files.
Required preparation and materials
• Validated Q1 finding
• Moderated Q2 capability register
• Published-experience register
• Action register and 30/60/90 plans
• Evidence index and sign-off sheet
Facilitator procedure
1. Read the draft answer to Question 1: verified days/times/use contexts, scope, variation,
causes and limitations.
2. Read the draft answer to Question 2: numbers/percentages Independent, Support Required
and Not Yet Demonstrated, plus evidence basis and limitations.
3. Check each material claim against the evidence index.
4. Confirm outstanding evidence, owner, deadline and whether it can change the conclusion.
5. Confirm annexure structure, version owner and report-drafting responsibility.
6. Obtain sign-off/acknowledgement from authorised representatives and explain the post-
workshop review dates.
Participant actions
• Confirm factual accuracy and responsibilities.
• Sign/acknowledge the findings and actions.
• Take ownership of follow-up evidence.
Required evidence and output
Evidence/output Minimum acceptance standard
Signed findings sheet Working answers to both FGC questions.
Action register Open items, owners and deadlines.
Report-ready evidence Traceable annexures and file references.
index
Follow-up calendar 30/60/90-day reviews and reassessments.
Facilitation cautions and decision rules
• Do not conceal evidence gaps; state how they limit the conclusion.
• Do not report a project-wide yes/no when colleges or lecturers differ materially.
Completion gate: Close the workshop only when the evidence index reconciles, responsibilities are
accepted and the next validation dates are recorded.

POST-WORKSHOP FACILITATOR ACTIONS
Protect the evidence trail and sustain verification
Within five working days
• Reconcile and freeze the workshop evidence index.
• Follow up missing items and record whether late evidence changes a finding.
• Finalise moderated lecturer classifications and published-experience register.
• Draft the two FGC responses with quantified evidence, variation and limitations.
• Issue formal actions to named owners and confirm reassessment dates.
At 30, 60 and 90 days
Review point Minimum facilitator check
30 days Activation actions started; targeted clinics delivered; due reassessments
completed; new evidence indexed.
60 days Usage compared with workshop baseline; content quality and support
barriers reviewed; missed actions escalated.
90 days Capability change and learner-use rhythm verified; licence decisions
executed where justified; FGC corrective-action update prepared.
Recommended report discipline
Question 1 construction: State the verified reporting period, predominant days/times and use
contexts, scope and variation, evidence sources, limited/non-use areas, causes and limitations.
Question 2 construction: State the number assessed and number/percentage in each capability
classification, the observed evidence basis, variation, limitations, support actions and reassessment
timetable.
Final reminder: The strongest report is not the most positive one; it is the one in which every material
statement can be traced to evidence and every gap has been disclosed and actioned.
